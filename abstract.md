# Building a Job Ads Database from Swedish Digitized Newspapers

Certain kinds of historical work — particularly women's, young people's, and
domestic servants’ — are poorly captured by conventional historical sources.
Newspaper job advertisements offer a complementary window into these labour
markets.

In our ambition to reconstruct the histories of these hidden occupations we
have created a prototype Job Ads Database (JADB). The job ads are drawn from
the Swedish National Library's newspaper corpus using proximity searches with
circa 150,000 occupation-title spelling variants from the Swedish HISCO
classification. Because the underlying corpus is not segmented into texts, we
have used a subset drawn from the Stockholm focused Dagens Nyheter, 1870--1970
to perform litmus tests. The data is well aligned with official unemployment
statistics, with some interesting exceptions and the small sample of hits
subjected to manual inspection were job advertisements.

We are at the point where we have established, within reasonable doubt, that
the newspaper corpus is a veritable cornucopia of labour market data and we are
ready to start disaggregating the corpus into individual job advertisements. In
this talk I will focus on the technological challenges we have, and are
foreseeing, working with this material and how we are dealing with them as we
scale up to map occupational titles, gender distributions and descriptions
across all digitized Swedish newspapers from the past 200 years.
